# Super-obstacles

## What
Each world ends with a bigger, special challenge:
- **Hedgehog** (meadow): a hedgehog shakes volleys of two or three leaves at once off its
  back toward Piko, each with its own word, a bit slower than in normal levels; the next
  volley comes when every leaf of the last one is saved or scored. Saved leaves fly back
  onto its back, goals are cheered as in the meadow. Several words at once train choosing
  a target without any failure. At the end it curls into a ball, rolls a happy circle
  and waves.
- **Fractured asteroid** (space): a big asteroid, when pushed, splits into smaller
  targets with shorter words.
- **Cracking boulder / rock labyrinth** (planet): precise typing cracks it into safe
  stepping stones; accuracy matters more than speed here.
- **Rolling energy wave** (dragons): countered by a rhythm sequence - short letter groups
  typed to a steady beat. Off-beat typing still counts, only less smoothly.
- **Friendly mole** (egg journey): pops up unexpectedly with a short word that rewards a
  quick reaction; missing it just makes the mole wave and hide.
- **Cable knot** (junkyard): every short word unties one loop; an exact word unties a
  whole loop, one with mistakes half of it.
- **Battery** (workshop) and **storm cloud** (rainy town): rhythm sequences like the
  energy wave; the battery fills four bars, the cloud shrinks and the sun comes out.
- **Main gate** (robot city): a long code split into short groups; each group lights one
  part of the gate's ring.
- **Family welcome** (robot home): little robots pop out of boxes with short words, like
  the friendly mole.
- None of them can fail the level.

## Why
- Variety keeps children engaged; low replay value is a top complaint about Dance Mat.
