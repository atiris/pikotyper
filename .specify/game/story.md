# Story

## What
The child travels through five worlds. Each world has several short levels and ends
with a super-obstacle and a reward that opens the next world. Short, skippable intro
scenes (a few lines of text and a simple animation) tell the story.

1. **Training Meadow** - insects and mice come toward a spider web hung between two
   flowers; a typed word lets Piko blow his dandelion, and the wind lifts the creature over
   the web (mice hop under it). A creature not typed in time tears a hole into the web and
   goes on happily: nobody is ever caught, and no spider comes. Hitting animals, even
   softly, is the wrong message for small children; helping them is better.
   Super-obstacle: a big spider web.
   Reward: a secret invitation to space; the child meets **Piko**.
2. **Space Rocket Flight** - typing rhythm drives the rocket speedometer; a soft energy
   beam pushes asteroids and planets out of the path. Super-obstacle: fractured asteroid.
   Reward: landing clearance.
3. **New Planet** - typing builds stepping stones across a swamp and picks the right path
   at road signs (each sign shows a word). Super-obstacle: cracking boulder / rock
   labyrinth. Reward: **Telekinesis (the Force)**.
4. **Dragon Domain** - the Force and gentle shield fields divert falling space rocks away
   from baby dragons. Super-obstacle: rolling energy wave. Reward: a **Dragon Egg**.
5. **Egg Journey** - the hatching egg rolls downhill; typing makes it jump over rocks and
   stumps. Super-obstacle: a friendly mole. Reward: a new companion hatches and
   **endless rescue missions** unlock (replayable, generated from all worlds).

- Worlds unlock in order; finished levels can always be replayed.
- On the map these five worlds form one named story ("Piko and the Dragon Egg"): its name
  stands above its world cards, left aligned with the first card, so later stories can be
  added as further rows.
- Every level can be finished by any child: obstacles that are not cleared in time just
  pass by or are helped by Piko; they never cause failure.

## Why
- The story gives a reason to practice without competition; replayable rescue missions
  give long-term variety without streak pressure.
